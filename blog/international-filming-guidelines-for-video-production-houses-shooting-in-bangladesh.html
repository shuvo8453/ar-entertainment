How to Plan an International Shoot in Bangladesh (2026 Guide: Permits, Crew, Costs & Support)

Bangladesh is becoming a powerful destination for international filmmakers, documentary teams, streaming platforms and global content creators. From tea gardens and river ports to dense megacities and rural villages, the country offers a rare mix of visual diversity and cost efficiency.

But to plan a successful international shoot in Bangladesh, you must clearly understand:

  • Filming permits and government approvals
  • Visa types for foreign crew
  • Drone regulations and airspace rules
  • Local crew and fixer support
  • Equipment import vs rental in Bangladesh
  • Safety, culture and logistics
  • Realistic production budgets and cost brackets

This 2026 guide walks you through each step and explains how Libanza Films can act as your local production partner in Bangladesh.

Film Fixer in Bangladesh

Why Bangladesh Is an Excellent Filming Destination

Bangladesh combines diverse locations, experienced crews and budget-friendly production costs. From Dhaka's urban life to the Sundarbans mangroves, Chattogram's port, Sylhet's tea gardens, and documentary-friendly villages, filmmakers get substantial visual variety with manageable permit processes and professional local support.

For documentaries, branded content, development communication and commercial shoots, Bangladesh offers:

  • High visual contrast within short travel distances
  • Cost-effective crews and production services
  • Strong experience with NGOs, INGOs and UN agencies
  • Access to both ultra-urban and deeply rural stories

Popular Filming Locations in Bangladesh (2026)

1. Dhaka City

  • Old Dhaka's heritage lanes and river life at Sadarghat
  • Korail and other dense communities for social documentaries
  • Modern business districts, flyovers and urban nightlife
  • Government buildings, universities and media houses

Dhaka is usually the entry point for visas, permits, crew and equipment.

2. Sundarbans

  • World's largest mangrove forest
  • Rivers, canals and fishing communities
  • Honey collectors and forest-dependent livelihoods

Perfect for environment, climate, wildlife and livelihood documentaries.

3. Chattogram & Hill Tracts

  • Port operations and container yards
  • Hill tracts, indigenous communities and rural markets
  • Long coastal strip and beaches for travel and brand visuals

4. Sylhet & Rural Villages

  • Lush tea gardens, haor landscapes and rivers
  • Waterfalls and forest edges for lifestyle and tourism narratives
  • Agriculture, cottage industries and river ghats across rural Bangladesh

Many NGO/INGO and TV documentary projects are filmed in these regions.

Essential Permits for Filming in Bangladesh (2026)

International shoots must comply with the applicable legal framework to avoid delays or the confiscation of footage. The exact process can differ by project type (news, docu, brand, NGO), but the overall structure is similar.

1. Primary Filming Permission

Usually processed through relevant government authorities such as:

  • Ministry of Information / Broadcasting
  • External publicity or related wings
  • Home Ministry or security agencies (for sensitive topics)

Purpose: legal permission to film in Bangladesh for most international projects.

Typical documents: project synopsis, schedule, locations, crew list, company profile and local partner details (e.g. Libanza Films).

Estimated processing time: 10 to 21 working days, depending on subject and location.

Visa & Drone Permit Requirements

2. Visa for International Crew (F-Type / Filming Visa)

Most foreign crews will require an F-Type (Filming) Visa or a Journalist Visa for pure news/documentary coverage.

Typical requirements include:

  • Approved filming permission or application reference
  • Passport copies and photos
  • Company letters and crew role descriptions
  • Tentative travel dates and filming locations

Estimated processing time: 7 to 15 days via respective embassies or missions.

3. Drone Permit (CAAB & Security Clearance)

Using a drone in Bangladesh requires formal approval from the Civil Aviation Authority of Bangladesh (CAAB) and, in some cases, air force or local security agencies.

You will typically need to share:

  • Drone type, serial number and technical details
  • Licensed pilot details and experience
  • Flight plan with location, altitude, date and time
  • Project purpose and synopsis

Estimated processing time: 7 to 10 working days for planned flights.

Location, Municipality & Security Support

4. Location & Municipality Permits

Additional permits are often required for:

  • Public roads, bridges, markets and parks
  • City corporation or municipality areas
  • Tourist locations and heritage zones

Libanza Films typically manages location permissions and fees for international clients as part of its film fixer and production support service.

5. Police & Security Support

Recommended when filming:

  • Large crowds, rallies or politically sensitive spaces
  • VIP movements and official events
  • Border areas or strategic installations

Local partners help arrange police liaison, crowd control and safety briefings for your crew.

Equipment Strategy: Import vs Local Rental

International productions can either bring their own camera package or rent inside Bangladesh.

Option 1: Bringing Your Own Equipment

Pros:

  • Complete familiarity with your gear and workflow
  • Pre-tested rigs, lenses and recording setups

Requirements and risks:

  • Temporary Import Permit (TIP) / carnet or equivalent
  • Detailed equipment list with serial numbers
  • Customs inspections and possible extra time at the airport
  • Risk of delay if paperwork is incomplete or unclear

Option 2: Renting Equipment Locally

Pros:

  • No customs hassle or carnet requirements
  • Faster on-ground deployment and replacements
  • Ability to scale up or modify the package mid-shoot

Available locally in Dhaka:

  • Cinema cameras (FX6/FX9, C300/C500, RED, etc.)
  • Drones, gimbals, LED lighting and grip
  • Audio recorders, wireless kits and boom systems

Libanza Films works with trusted rental partners in Dhaka to offer production-ready camera, lighting and sound packages.

Hiring Local Crew & Fixer in Bangladesh (2026)

A strong local fixer and crew are the most critical success factors for foreign productions in Bangladesh.

Key Local Roles

  • Fixer / Line Producer and Production Manager
  • Director, Assistant Directors and DOP
  • Drone team, sound recordist, light and grip crew
  • Translator / interpreter and local researcher
  • Art director, wardrobe and stylist (for commercials)
  • Editor, colourist and sound designer (for local post-production)

Why a Local Fixer in Bangladesh Is Essential

A professional fixer will:

  • Coordinate all permits and government papers
  • Handle crew hiring, payroll and local contracts
  • Arrange transport, accommodation and local SIMs
  • Manage locations, permissions and timing
  • Advise on cultural sensitivity and topics to avoid
  • Provide real-time solutions when plans change on the ground

Libanza Films operates as a full-scope fixer and production partner, not just a translator or runner.

Standard International Shoot Workflow in Bangladesh

Phase 1 — Pre-Production

  • Initial consultation and feasibility check
  • Permit and visa planning for all crew members
  • Budget estimate and cost breakdown
  • Location research and remote / on-ground scouting
  • Drone and security clearances
  • Crew and equipment planning
  • Logistics mapping (travel, boats, roads, seasons, tides)

Phase 2 — Production

  • Airport welcome and local onboarding
  • On-ground filming with local and foreign crew
  • Drone operations and legal compliance
  • Daily schedule adjustments for weather and traffic
  • Local language support and translation
  • Data backup, safety and risk management

Phase 3 — Post-Production (Optional in Bangladesh)

If you choose to finish post-production in Bangladesh, Libanza Films can support:

  • Offline and online editing
  • Colour grading and sound design
  • Subtitles (Bangla and English)
  • Motion graphics, maps and data visualisations
  • Deliverables in broadcast, web and OTT formats

Cost of International Filming in Bangladesh (2026)

International filming in Bangladesh typically costs USD 2,000 to 25,000+, depending on project type, crew size, shoot duration, locations, drone use, permits, and post-production. Documentary shoots are at the lower end of this range; commercial and multi-location campaigns sit at the higher end.

1. Documentary & NGO/INGO Shoots (Approx. USD 2,000–7,000)

Typical inclusions:

  • Local fixer and small crew
  • Transport within one region
  • Basic camera and sound package
  • Location permits for non-sensitive areas
  • Basic editing or raw footage handover

2. Branded Content / NGO Films / Corporate Stories (Approx. USD 5,000–12,000)

  • Larger crew and multiple locations
  • Drone work and advanced cinematography
  • Script and storyboard support
  • Professional edit, colour and sound
  • Delivery for web, NGO reports or campaigns

3. Commercial Productions / International Ads (Approx. USD 10,000–25,000+)

  • Full production unit with casting and talents
  • Studio or controlled set builds
  • High-end equipment and multiple shoot days
  • Agency collaboration and international client servicing

Note: costs will vary by brief, so early consultation is essential.

Safety, Culture & Compliance Checklist

To keep your shoot safe and respectful in Bangladesh, you should:

  • Follow all drone and aviation laws
  • Avoid filming sensitive locations without proper permits
  • Respect cultural and religious norms at all times
  • Obtain consent before filming vulnerable communities
  • Plan for Dhaka traffic and seasonal weather (monsoon, floods)
  • Build at least one buffer day into your schedule

Libanza Films helps plan safe routes, realistic timings and community permissions, especially for NGO and development work.

Common Mistakes International Filmmakers Should Avoid

  • Arriving without written permits — verbal approval is not enough.
  • Hiring random fixers without references — can lead to legal, safety or budget issues.
  • Ignoring drone regulations — can result in drone seizure or forced landing.
  • Over-optimistic schedules — Dhaka traffic, weather and river travel need buffer time.
  • Underestimating post-production needs — subtitles, translations and local context are essential.
  • Not planning for sound — Bangladesh is noisy; you need a strong on-location audio strategy.
