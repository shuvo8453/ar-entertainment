Film Fixer in Cox's Bazar for International Productions

Libanza Films provides professional film fixer services in Cox's Bazar for international documentary filmmakers, broadcasters, journalists, NGOs, environmental organisations and foreign production houses. Film fixer support starts from USD 2,000. Ministry of Information filming permits take 10 to 21 working days. RRRC permits for Rohingya camp access take 2 to 3 additional weeks and are applied for in parallel with the MoI permit. Named Cox's Bazar productions supported include the documentary by María José, an independent audiovisual producer from Ecuador, for whom Libanza Films provided full on-ground production support including coastal location access, community liaison and logistics.

Cox's Bazar is globally recognised for the world's longest natural sea beach, rich coastal ecosystems, fishing communities, the largest refugee settlement on earth, and proximity to ecologically sensitive marine zones. These elements make it the most internationally documented location in Bangladesh. Filming here requires specialised local knowledge: coastal regulations, marine safety, weather dependency, layered authority permissions and community coordination make a professional local film fixer essential for any foreign production.

Why Cox's Bazar Is a Prime Location for International Shooting

Coastal and Marine Visual Diversity

Cox's Bazar offers beaches, fishing harbours, coastal villages, tourism zones, marine transport routes and river-sea intersections. This diversity supports documentaries, climate films, tourism campaigns and humanitarian storytelling at a scale and visual quality unavailable anywhere else in Bangladesh.

Environmental and Climate Storytelling Hub

The region is the primary Bangladesh location for climate change, disaster resilience, marine conservation and humanitarian narratives. NGOs, INGOs, UN agencies and international broadcasters regularly produce here.

Strategic Access to Southern Bangladesh

Cox's Bazar serves as the gateway to southern coastal regions, islands and marine zones, making it operationally strategic for multi-location international shoots. Dhaka to Cox's Bazar is 45 minutes by domestic flight.

Why You Need a Film Fixer in Cox's Bazar

Cox's Bazar is not an open filming zone for foreign crews. Beach areas, marine routes, refugee-adjacent zones, ports and coastal communities each require permissions from different authorities. A crew that arrives without pre-arranged permits and a local fixer will be stopped before filming begins.

Libanza Films acts as your local authority liaison, coastal logistics planner and on-ground fixer — preventing delays, access issues and regulatory risks while keeping your production schedule intact.

Filming in the Rohingya Camps: The RRRC Permit Process

Cox's Bazar is home to the world's largest refugee settlement. Kutupalong and Balukhali in Ukhiya upazila host over one million refugees, making this the most internationally documented humanitarian story in South Asia. Access for foreign film crews requires a permit from the Refugee Relief and Repatriation Commissioner (RRRC) — a separate government authority from the Ministry of Information, with its own application process and approval criteria.

The RRRC permit is issued alongside — not instead of — the standard Ministry of Information coastal filming clearance. Both must be in hand before filming inside the camps begins.

What the RRRC Application Requires

  • Full project description including editorial purpose and intended distribution
  • Complete crew list with passport details and organisational affiliations
  • Specific camp blocks or areas where filming is planned (GPS coordinates preferred)
  • Safeguarding protocol documentation for filming with vulnerable communities
  • Consent procedures for filming individual refugees, including children
  • Ministry of Information permit (applied for in parallel — not required first)
  • NGO or UN agency letter of support (strongly recommended — significantly aids approval)

RRRC Permit Practical Realities

Processing Time

2 to 3 weeks from complete application submission. Incomplete applications restart the clock. Libanza Films prepares the full documentation pack before submitting to avoid processing delays.

Approval Assessment

Approval is not automatic. RRRC assesses editorial content, organisational credibility and safeguarding approach. Productions with humanitarian or development purposes are more readily approved than investigative or adversarial editorial approaches.

Government Escort

Government liaison officers may accompany filming inside camp areas. Libanza Films coordinates this liaison and briefs international crews on appropriate conduct and communication with escort officers during the shoot.

Community Liaison

The RRRC permit gives legal access to camp areas. It does not give access to individual refugees. Libanza Films coordinates community liaison including NGO introductions, community leader engagement and translators with Rohingya dialect familiarity.

Safeguarding Requirements — Not Optional

Any production filming inside the camps must comply with:

  • Individual informed consent from every person filmed, in a language they understand, explained by a qualified interpreter
  • Specific consent for filming children — parental consent required and in some cases NGO child protection officer involvement
  • No filming that could identify individuals to Myanmar authorities or put family members still in Myanmar at risk
  • Data protection for all footage involving identifiable individuals, covering storage, transmission and final editorial use

Libanza Films provides pre-production safeguarding briefing for all camp productions and can arrange child protection officer consultation.

Location-Specific Filming Challenges in Cox's Bazar

  • Unpredictable weather and tidal conditions affecting shoot windows

  • Security-sensitive areas requiring police or naval presence
  • Seasonal tourism crowd pressure at peak beach locations
  • Limited daylight windows for marine shooting — pre-dawn to morning only for fishing harbour content
  • Logistics constraints outside Cox's Bazar town for Ukhiya and Teknaf camp-based productions
  • Drone flight restrictions around the airport, naval installations and camp perimeters
